Why this work

I know what it means to build, and rebuild, a career.

My own path has crossed economics, technology, product leadership and higher education. I began as an economist, built products and led global teams in the technology industry, then moved into academic leadership, where I helped institutions and people respond to changing professions, technologies and expectations.

That path was not linear. It asked me to recognize transferable strengths, step into unfamiliar rooms, make hard choices, and keep learning. It is also why I believe a career is not something you find once. It is something you learn to shape on purpose.

Own your journey.

Owning your journey means becoming an agent for yourself. You go looking for the insight, the options and the strategy, instead of waiting for them to arrive. The work moves through three steps.

01 / See clearly

Understand where you actually stand.

Your strengths, your motivations, your circumstances, and the realities of the environment around you.

02 / Choose strategically

Decide what deserves your time.

Identify the credible possibilities, weigh the tradeoffs, and commit your energy and reputation where they count.

03 / Move deliberately

Turn the decision into progress.

Positioning, relationships, action, and steady forward motion. After each step we check whether it took you where you needed to go, and adjust.

The aim is not simply to feel more confident. It is to see your situation more clearly, make stronger choices, and act with greater agency.

A real world roadmap, built together. The journey stays yours.

The distinction

Career planning, not just professional development.

Professional development is the certificates: project management, analytics, the skills you choose to learn. Radhika values those. She used them. Career planning is the layer above: who you know, what you know, and what you need to learn next.

At Bell Labs she moved from the economic analysis group into a technology group knowing no technology. The company sent her to programming training on campus, then to a company on the West Coast for two weeks to learn object oriented programming. It changed her work. Intentional planning, sometimes a little serendipity, the right people around you, and training exactly when you need it.
