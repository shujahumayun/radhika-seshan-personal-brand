About

I have changed direction more than once.

That is where the work comes from. Economist, technology and product leader, academic leader, and now coach and adviser. Every one of those moves meant walking into a room where my experience did not yet have a name, and working out how to make it count.

01

Economist and international professional

Where the habit of mind came from.

I was trained to read facts, people, and the structures they sit inside.

I trained as an economist and finished a PhD. What that really gave me was less a subject than a way of looking. Read the facts. Read the people. Read the structures they work inside. Then ask how all three are going to move.

I worked internationally early, which taught me the other half of it. The same facts get read differently depending on the room you are standing in. I have used that in every job since, and it is the first thing I bring to a client's situation.

02

Technology and product leader

The move that taught me what the work is.

I moved into technology knowing no technology.

At Bell Labs I moved from the economic analysis group into a technology group. I knew none of it. The company sent me to programming training on campus, then to a company on the West Coast for two weeks to learn object oriented programming. It changed my work.

The years after that were spent in product management, with global teams across the US and the UK, living through technological disruption from the inside rather than reading about it afterwards. That is why I do not treat change as an abstraction when a client is standing in the middle of one.

“Intentional planning, sometimes a little serendipity, the right people around you, and training exactly when you need it.”
03

Academic and organizational leader

Leading institutions, and the adults inside them.

Change at institutional scale has to be led, not announced.

I moved into higher education and then into senior academic leadership, at Northeastern University, at San Diego State University, and at a large public university in Minnesota. Deanships, new portfolios, and organizations big enough that a decision only becomes real once people are brought with it.

My work has centered on adult and professional learners, and on what experienced people actually need: relevance, flexibility, support, belonging, and a credible connection between effort and opportunity. The daily job is aligning people, technology, learning, student experience and financial sustainability around shared priorities.

04

Coach, adviser and guide

Why all of it now points here.

Every one of those moves needed something I rarely had.

Someone who had seen the route before. Someone who would help me choose one, and who would ask afterwards whether it took me where I needed to go. Intentional planning, the right people around me, and training exactly when I needed it were what made the moves work. When they were missing, the moves cost more than they should have.

That is the work now. Career planning for professionals ready to take the next step, guided strategy for groups, and advisory for organizations preparing their people for change. Not counseling. Not motivation. Not theories. A real world roadmap, built together, with the journey staying yours.
