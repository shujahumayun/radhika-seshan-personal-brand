Career strategy

Your next career may already be hidden in your current one

Sample pieceSix minute read

Sample text. This page exists to show the reading layout. Every word below is placeholder and will be replaced by Radhika's own writing.

Most people describe a career change as leaving. You leave the field, you leave the title, you leave the version of yourself that other people had learned to expect. Described that way it sounds like a loss, which is one reason so many capable people put it off for years.

In practice the changes that work are rarely departures. They are recombinations. Something you were already doing quietly, alongside the job you were paid for, turns out to be the thing that carries. The move is not inventing a new self. It is noticing which parts of the current one are already load bearing.

The part no course teaches

Professional development is the certificates: project management, analytics, the skills you choose to learn. I value those. I used them. Career planning is the layer above, and it is made of three things. Who you know. What you know. What you need to learn next.

The reason that layer stays invisible is that nobody assigns it. No manager schedules a review of whether your destination is still the right one. No program tells you that what worked in your last role will not work in the next. You have to go looking, which is what owning your journey actually means.

“Intentional planning, sometimes a little serendipity, the right people around you, and training exactly when you need it.”

An example from my own record

At Bell Labs I moved from the economic analysis group into a technology group knowing no technology. What made that survivable was not confidence. It was that the company sent me to programming training on campus, then to a company on the West Coast for two weeks to learn object oriented programming. Training arrived at the moment I needed it, and it changed my work.

The economics did not get left behind. It became the thing that made me useful in a technical room, because I could read the structures and the people as well as the product. That is the recombination. Nothing was discarded.

Where to start

Take the work you did in the last year and separate it from your job title. List what you were actually doing when the work went well. Then ask which of those things someone else would pay for on purpose, rather than as a by product of the role you happen to hold.

That list is usually the beginning of the route. It is rarely the whole of it, which is why the next step is a conversation with someone who has seen the terrain, and who will ask afterwards whether the step took you where you needed to go.
